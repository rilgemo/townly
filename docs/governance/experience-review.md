# Experience Review

Use this review before implementing a new gameplay feature. Its purpose is to clarify the experience Townly is creating before code, content, or systems make the direction harder to change.

Keep answers brief. A feature does not need to satisfy every question, but unclear answers are a reason to reduce scope or reconsider the proposal.

## Reality

- What does the player actually do in this place?
- Why does this possibility exist in Willow Village?
- Does the result feel physical and believable rather than abstract?

## Player Experience

- What will the player feel, notice, or understand for the first time?
- Is the player experiencing the village or operating a system?
- Why would this moment matter to them?

## Restoration

- Does this bring back a way of living, or merely unlock a feature?
- What existed before, and why was it lost?
- Who remembers what its return would mean?

## Discovery

- What must the player observe or learn before this appears?
- Does the interface reveal only what the player knows?
- Is discovery experienced before it is explained?

## Belonging

- How does this make the player feel less like a visitor?
- How do residents acknowledge the player's presence naturally?
- Does this deepen attachment to Willow Village?

## World Change

- What becomes visibly or meaningfully different in the village?
- Will residents notice, remember, or use the change?
- Does the result remain present after the action ends?

## Environmental Storytelling

- Can the place itself communicate history or change?
- What detail can replace a system notification or exposition?
- Does the environment show that life existed before the player?

## Simplicity

- Can this experience use existing mechanics and state?
- What is the smallest version that proves the idea?
- Can any proposed system, label, or abstraction be removed?
