# Shelter Identity

## Player Experience

Your Shelter is the first place in Willow Village that may slowly become home through the player's lived history.

The village does not give the player a finished house. It offers an abandoned room: old floorboards, worn walls, empty corners, and enough protection to rest. Its emptiness is meaningful because anything that appears there later should have a story.

## World Change

At this milestone, the shelter does not gain furniture, capacity, or levels. Its identity becomes clear:

- It is a physical room the player may return to.
- It is usable but abandoned and empty.
- The player may observe it, rest there, and leave.
- It contains no abstract storage or upgrade interface.

The change is not that the room becomes mechanically stronger. The player begins recognizing it as their own place in Willow Village.

## Core Principle

Shelter is a lived place, not a system menu.

It must never default into:

- an inventory container
- storage slots or capacity
- a crafting station
- a furniture catalogue
- a housing level
- an abstract upgrade tree

Resources remain physical ideas, but this milestone does not implement carrying limits or storage.

## Future Compatibility

Future improvements should arise from actions and relationships. Objects gain meaning through how they were obtained, who helped make them, and why the player chose to place them here.

An old craftsperson should not exist to unlock recipes. They should remember and pass on ways of living that Willow Village has nearly lost.

This direction leaves room for beds, chests, work surfaces, repairs, and decoration without defining any of those systems yet.

## Completion Test

Shelter Identity succeeds when:

- The room feels abandoned, simple, empty, and usable.
- The only available living actions are looking around, resting, and leaving.
- No furniture object exists yet.
- No inventory, storage, crafting, housing, tool, or time system is introduced.
- Existing exploration and restoration behavior remains unchanged.
