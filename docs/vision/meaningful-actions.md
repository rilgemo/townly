# Meaningful Actions

## Player Experience

The player should understand how something was obtained because they chose a concrete action in a real place.

The first Wood is not a location payout. It is a handful of fallen branches found beneath damp leaves and carried back to Willow Village.

## World Change

At this foundation stage, actions change the player's practical relationship with familiar places:

- Forest becomes somewhere to search for fallen wood or examine useful plants.
- Mine becomes somewhere to recover usable stone from abandoned rubble.
- Repeated careful visits reveal paths and possibilities that a location menu could not express.

## Action Principle

A place provides opportunities. An action determines the outcome.

```text
Forest
  → Search among fallen branches
  → Examine wild plants

Mine
  → Search through loose rubble
```

Wood and Herb are not automatically obtained together. Stone is found through a concrete action rather than granted by entering Mine.

Future capabilities may add new actions—such as cutting timber with an axe—but this milestone does not implement tools, equipment, durability, crafting, or professions.

## Completion Test

Meaningful Actions succeeds when:

- Forest still provides Wood and Herb through separate choices.
- Mine still provides Stone through a specific choice.
- Result text explains what the player did and what they found.
- No location automatically grants a fixed bundle of materials.
- Existing restoration costs and resource totals continue working.
